# platform loop retro r6 — Claude Opus 5 (1M context)

## Summary (≤5 lines)
Rounds are not burned on red CI (5% of round-entries) — they are burned on reviewer round-trips: 53% of all round-entries ended clean **and** CI-green, then another round ran anyway. The cure already exists in the corpus (the oracle-fleet §Maturity disposition rule, ported to circles for assembly PRs) and was never ported to homelab's ordinary-PR rubric. Separately, the ADR-127 round-cap alignment 3→5 landed in `coordinator-scan.sh` on 2026-09-11 but never reached `estimate_budget.py`, which still sizes every session for 3 rounds — the three rows that came within 3% of their cap all ran 5–6. First touch fails on 61% of tasks, concentrated on the un-pinned base deepseek id. Ledger caveat, said once: `reviewer_rounds`, `wall_time_s` decomposition, `retry_storms` and `$0.00` costs are unreliable per the brief; every count below is from trails or from round-level fields.

## Findings (ranked, ≤6)

### F1 — Reviewer blocking-set non-convergence is where the rounds go, and the fix is already written in another repo
**Evidence.** 84 of 158 round-entries (53%) were `clean` + CI `true` yet followed by another round; only 8 (5%) were `ci-failed`. Per task: homelab#1041 — 5 rounds, 5/5 clean+green, 0 red, 4 `CHANGES_REQUESTED`; homelab#1268 — 7 rounds, 7/7 clean+green, 0 red, 3 CR; homelab#1151 — 6 rounds, 6/6 clean+green, 0 red. Across the 12 homelab rows with a readable review trail: **29 `CHANGES_REQUESTED` verdicts drove 65 rounds** (5.4 rounds/task). homelab#913/PR#915 is the shape in one PR — 7 rounds, 81 commits, 20 h, and each verdict a *fresh disjoint* blocking set: 18:40 "Two blocking findings" → 19:34 "Two BLOCKING findings, both in-diff defects in this PR's own fix" → 19:58 partial → 20:59 "only half-landed" → 08-26 07:45 a new BLOCKING finding against a hoist an earlier round had introduced → 14:24 APPROVED. Exactly one of those seven is a §Maturity BLOCKING class.
**Mechanism (falsifiable).** homelab's `.agents/review.md` has `## BLOCKING` and `## NOT blocking — follow-ups, **sparingly**` — a blocking-by-default posture, so each fix round exposes the next layer instead of converging. oracle-fleet cured this with §Maturity (BLOCKING = literal secret · committed blob · CI-red on the diff · a regression the child reviews missed; **everything else → follow-up issue inside an APPROVING review**); circles adopted it via PR#79 (merged 2026-08-12) into `.agents/review-goal.md`, explicitly citing "retro r3 F2 … each fix surfaces a fresh disjoint blocking set". homelab has **no** `review-goal.md` and `.agents/review.md` never received the narrowing. Falsify by classifying the 29 CR verdicts: if the rubric is the cause, post-round-2 findings are dominated by classes §Maturity routes to follow-up.
**Process change.** Add a `§Maturity — merge-forward` disposition block to **`.agents/review.md`** (homelab), copying circles `.agents/review-goal.md:39–53` verbatim and scoping it to ordinary agent PRs.
**Expected saving.** Rail 3 (worker+reviewer rides, amortized — cents): on the observed mix, collapsing post-round-2 blocking sets removes ≈14 of 65 rounds (~22%) across 12 tasks. Rail 1 by named class: the *mid-loop human codeowner `CHANGES_REQUESTED`* (homelab#913, `RasmusSoot` 2026-08-25T20:37Z) — an out-of-sitting summons, ~€20–30, observed once per ~12 tasks.

### F2 — The ADR-127 3→5 round-cap alignment reached the scan but not the estimator
**Evidence.** `agents/coordinator-scan.sh:5022` now reads `RED_MAX="${RED_ROUNDS_MAX:-5}"  # 3→5 with the logic-round cap (ADR-127, 2026-09-11)`. `agents/estimate_budget.py:58` still reads `DEFAULT_ROUNDS = 3  # max review rounds before escalating`. Reality is neither: **9 of 33 structured tasks (27%) exceeded round 5**, oracle-fleet#397 reached round 9. The three rows that landed within 3% of their cap all ran 5–6 rounds — homelab#1041 (sm, $0.5245/$0.50 = **105%**, `calibration_error` 1.049), homelab#1205 (sm, $0.4998/$0.50 = **100%**), homelab#1151 (xs, $0.2415/$0.25 = **97%**) — while every row that ran ≤4 rounds landed at 4–39% of cap. Three `budget-403` strikes followed (homelab#778 r4, #892 r1, #1151 r1). The 3→5 drift is visible in the trail too: PR#1541 and PR#1545 carry `ARBITRATE (ci-red, FU-115) … (cap 3)` on 09-09 and `… (cap 5)` on 09-11, and the three PRs held since 09-08 flushed within 30 min of that change.
**Mechanism (falsifiable).** `estimate_cost` is `requests_per_round(issue_tokens) × rounds × …`; at `rounds=3` the point estimate is 1.67–3× low for the tail, so tail tasks select one tier too low. r4 F5's response (2026-09-14) doubled the *enforced* caps (`TIERS` xs 0.25→0.50 … lg 2.00→4.00) while leaving selection ceilings and `DEFAULT_ROUNDS` untouched — headroom restored by weakening the breaker the module exists to be (docstring: one run ate a $5.79 weekly budget). Falsify: re-run `pick_tier` over the ledger at `rounds=5`; if the three near-cap rows move up a tier, rounds was the mis-set input, not buffer.
**Process change.** One line: `DEFAULT_ROUNDS = 3` → `5` in **`agents/estimate_budget.py:58`**, with the existing `--self-test` `pick_tier` assertions (≈:851–854) updated in the same diff. Record — do not action here — that this makes the r4 F5 cap-doubling redundant at 5/3 × ½ = 0.83× today's sizing.
**Expected saving.** Rail 2 (billed dollars) and rail 3 only — no rail-1 claim. Prevents the 3 observed `budget-403` strikes (3 dead pods + 3 model swaps per 33 tasks) and restores the sm breaker to ~$0.50 while still covering both over-cap rows, which needed 1.05× and 1.00× of the old cap against 1.67× of headroom.

### F3 — First touch fails on 61% of tasks, on the one model the loop reaches for first
**Evidence.** 20 of 33 structured tasks (61%) had their first attempt end non-clean. By first-touch model: `deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash` **12/18 (67%)**, `haiku` 3/9 (33%), `opencode-go/deepseek-v4-flash` 2/2. Lifetime non-clean rate: base deepseek **26/59 (44%)** vs haiku **9/81 (11%)**. The provider-pinned `deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash:exacto` is **0/6**. The classes concentrate on the base id: `http-401-storm` 6/6, `repetition-loop` 5/5, `goose-32602-truncation` 7/9.
**Mechanism (falsifiable).** `:exacto` pins provider routing (`estimate_budget.py` docstring: "`:exacto` pins provider routing, never price"), so pinning removes the provider variance these classes track. Falsify by stratifying: all 6 exacto rounds are oracle-fleet and late (09-16/09-17), so this is **directional, not proven** — a stack or calendar effect is not yet excluded.
**Process change.** Do **not** open a new issue: post these rows as the ledger half of the read already waiting on them — **homelab#1669** (OPEN, Goal #1640 acceptance 8, "the exacto flip (PR#1639) re-read against a week of strike rows — standing or reverted").
**Expected saving.** Rail 2/3. At haiku's first-touch rate the same 33 tasks cost 11 dead first rides instead of 20 — **9 fewer dead pods + 9 fewer swaps per 33 tasks (27%)**.

### F4 — `goose-32602-truncation` is still the #1 error class, 7 events after the fix that was meant to retire it
**Evidence.** 9 of 38 non-clean round-entries — the largest single error class in the ledger. homelab#256 (retro r3 F1, titled "*the solved class still killing rounds*") set `GOOSE_MAX_TOKENS=16384` on the worker goose arm and closed 2026-08-11. **Seven of the nine post-date it**: homelab#791 (08-23), oracle-fleet#272 (08-26), sleep-tracking#123 (08-30, ×2), homelab#1205 (09-05), oracle-fleet#397 (09-13, ×2). The other two (circles#32, circles#19) are work dated 08-06/08-07, pre-fix.
**Mechanism (falsifiable).** JSON-RPC `-32602` is **Invalid params** — raised against the *request the harness sends*, so the oversize payload is a tool **result** being fed back in, not the model's completion. `GOOSE_MAX_TOKENS` bounds the completion budget and therefore cannot bound it; r3 F1 fixed a real wiring gap but not this class. Falsify by capturing one `-32602` session's failing request: if the oversized member is a `params`/tool-result field rather than a completion, the output cap was never the lever.
**Process change.** File ONE `agent-fix` issue against **`agents/agent-session.sh`** (goose arm) to cap tool-**result** size and re-classify the error as request-side. Parent = **homelab#256** (the origin issue this is derived from). Dedup first against homelab#1642 (`fleet-strike: error_class=unknown`) per the brief's filing discipline.
**Expected saving.** Rail 2/3 — 7 dead rides + 7 model swaps in the window (~1 per 4.7 tasks). oracle-fleet#397 burned 35,975 s active across 10 rounds carrying two of them.

### F5 — Half the deep-dive budget is unauditable, and the rank still can't see it
**Evidence.** Of the 8 pain-ranked deep-dive rows: ranks 2 & 7 (sleep-tracking) and 4 & 6 (oracle-fleet) live in repos that resolve for **neither** the default token nor `RETRO_GH_TOKEN` (`Could not resolve to a Repository`, both tokens, all four probes); rank 8 (homelab#778) is an operator-directed fan-out **pilot**, not a loop task; ranks 1 & 5 are merged successes still mislabelled `agent/blocked`. **One slot of eight (rank 3, homelab#1041) was an ordinary blocked loop task.**
**Mechanism (falsifiable).** homelab#1911 (r5, closed 09-23) added a bounded live-state re-read to `agents/retro-rank.py` that drops CLOSED-with-merged-PR candidates — the merged-success half. Reachability is the other half and is unguarded: a candidate whose repo the retro token cannot read is kept and silently consumes a deep-dive slot. Falsify: run `retro-rank.py` with this pod's token; if ranks 2/4/6/7 still survive into the top-8, the probe has no reachability arm.
**Process change.** Extend the probe **#1911 just added to `agents/retro-rank.py`**: a candidate whose repo probe returns "Could not resolve" is excluded and counted in the existing `… excluded N merged-work rows from the rank` line (no silent caps), and the rank pulls the next candidate.
**Expected saving.** Rail 1 — the retro is an operator-facing artifact; 4 of 8 slots returning "could not verify" wastes the deep-dive half of a retro run, ~E (€20–30) of sitting time per run.
