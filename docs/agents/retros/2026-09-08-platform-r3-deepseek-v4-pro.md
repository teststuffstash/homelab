# platform loop retro r3 — deepseek-v4-flash (goose)

## Summary (≤5 lines)
40 ledger rows: 21 agent/done, 19 agent/blocked. Total tracked spend $6.91 across 157 worker rounds. Half (20/40) of tasks needed ≥5 worker rounds; 40% (16/40) failed on the first round — deepseek-v4-flash alone accounts for 14 first-round failures. Reviewer iterative-revelation (7× CHANGES_REQUESTED on one PR) is the dominant round-burner, costing more cycles than model failures or CI-red. The retro-batch platform-r1 (#949) and platform-r2 (#1101) remain open with no merged process changes.

## Findings (ranked, ≤6)

### F1: Reviewer iterative-revelation amplifies round counts (rank: costliest)
- **Evidence**: homelab#913 PR #915: 14 total reviews, 7 CHANGES_REQUESTED by the reviewer bot spread across 4 time-separated sessions (Aug 25 18:40 → 18:40 → 19:34 → 19:58 → 20:59 → Aug 26 07:45). Each CHANGES_REQUESTED triggered a new worker round (rounds 3–11 in trail). The reviewer COMMENTED then immediately CHANGES_REQUESTED in the same review submission, discovering new issues iteratively rather than comprehensively. homelab#1041 PR #1058: same pattern — 3 CHANGES_REQUESTED across 4 reviews (Aug 30 20:53 → 21:26 → 22:02). homelab#625 PR #631: 2 CHANGES_REQUESTED across 5 reviews. In all cases, the second CHANGES_REQUESTED finding existed in the original diff — it was not introduced by the fix for finding #1.
- **Mechanism**: The reviewer recipe has no "second pass" requirement. When the reviewer finds one blocking issue, it posts COMMENTED + CHANGES_REQUESTED and stops. The worker fixes only what was named, pushes, and the reviewer scans again — finding a *different* blocking issue that was present from round 1. This serializes what should be a single review pass with all blocking findings enumerated.
- **Process change**: Amend the reviewer recipe (`review-goal.md`): after identifying one blocking issue, the reviewer MUST re-scan the complete diff for additional blocking findings before posting its final verdict. Add a `--comprehensive` scan flag: when set, the reviewer iterates over every changed file + every rubric rule before emitting CHANGES_REQUESTED. The cost is one extra model inference pass per review cycle.
- **Expected saving**: homelab#913: collapses 7 review round-trips (rounds 3–9) into ~2, saving 5 worker rounds + 5 reviewer rides. At subscription-amortized rates (haiku, rail 3 cash-amortized ≈ cents/ride), the direct spend saving is under $0.50. The real saving is **operator time**: each escalation through rounds triggered a 2026-08-25 20:37 human CHANGES_REQUESTED from RasmusSoot, and the final round-11 park (2026-08-26 07:53) consumed the operator's sit-down context. At €25/sit-down (rail 1), 1 avoided escalation saves ~€25. Fleet-wide: 4 verified tasks with this pattern (homelab#913, #1041, #625, #1439); conservatively 2 avoided escalations per retro cycle → ~€50/cycle. Confidence: HIGH — evidence from GitHub review timelines on 4 distinct PRs.

### F2: deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash first-round failure rate drives round inflation
- **Evidence**: 14 of 16 first-round failures are on deepseek-v4-flash (87.5%). Error classes: nonzero-exit-1 (×4: homelab#913, #625, #866, #617), goose-32602-truncation (×3: homelab#1205, circles#32, circles#19), budget-exhausted-key (×3: homelab#1151, oracle-fleet#284, homelab#779), repetition-loop (×5: oracle-fleet#304), no-output (×1: homelab#1149). Compare: haiku had 60/69 clean runs (87%) vs deepseek-v4-flash's 42/67 clean runs (63%). The strike mechanism fires correctly for these (the ledger shows model swaps from deepseek→haiku), but every first-round failure consumes a round-0 that could have been productive.
- **Mechanism**: deepseek-v4-flash is serving as the default first-round model but has a 37% non-clean rate. The harness correctly swaps to haiku after a strike, but the wasted round burns compute and wall time. The truncation errors (goose-32602) suggest context-window exhaustion on complex issues; nonzero-exit-1 suggests process crashes within the model provider.
- **Process change**: In the coordinator brief (`README.md` §State machine), add a cold-start heuristic: if an issue's body/title exceeds ~2K characters (estimated), skip deepseek-v4-flash as the first-round model and start with haiku directly. The heuristic lives in `estimate_budget.py` as a `--no-cold-start-model` flag that the coordinator reads before dispatching round 1. Alternatively, flip the default first-round model to haiku (87% clean rate) and reserve deepseek-v4-flash for explicit `agent/tool/large` opt-in.
- **Expected saving**: Avoids 16 first-round failures per 40 tasks. At openrouter billed rate for deepseek-v4-flash (~$0.02–0.05 per failed 1st round), direct saving ≈ $0.50–1.00 per cycle (rail 2). Each avoided round saves ~2–5 min of active wall time before the strike fires. Confidence: MEDIUM — the heuristic (body length > 2K chars) needs validation against actual truncation thresholds; the nonzero-exit-1 cases are provider-side and unpredictable.

### F3: http-401 auth-storm on oracle-fleet deepseek-v4-flash without model swap
- **Evidence**: 6 http-401-storm events across 3 oracle-fleet tasks, all on deepseek-v4-flash via OpenRouter: #272 (1 event, Aug 26), #279 (3 events on the same round-1, Aug 30), #273 (2 events on rounds 1 and 3, Aug 30). oracle-fleet#279's 3 storms triggered retry_storms=3 (harness-level retries) but the harness kept using the *same model* — the strike/swap path never activated. oracle-fleet#279 consumed 84.4h wall time (83.9h idle) with 3 of 5 rounds wasted on auth-storms.
- **Mechanism**: http-401-storm is an OpenRouter authentication/authorization failure specific to deepseek-v4-flash on the oracle stack. The coordinator's STRIKE clause (§invariants) says "infra failures are strikes that swap the model instead of consuming a round" — but auth-storm appears to bypass this classification because it's an API-level error, not a harness death. The retry_storms counter confirms the harness is retrying without model swap.
- **Process change**: Add `http-401-storm` explicitly to the strike error-class list in the coordinator brief's STRIKE clause. When the worker exit_status is `auth-storm` with error_class `http-401-storm`, the coordinator MUST count it as a strike (swap model, do not consume a logic round). Additionally, add a same-model gate: if the same `(model, error_class=401)` pair fires twice in one issue's lifetime, escalate to `agent/error` with an AGENT_ERROR comment per the STRIKE-channel path — don't retry a third time.
- **Expected saving**: oracle-fleet#279: 3 storms collapsed to 1 → saves 2 wasted attempts, $0.04 in billed OpenRouter calls (rail 2), 2× wall-idle windows reduced. oracle-fleet#273: 2 storms collapsed to 1. Saves ~$0.06 total per occurrence. No operator time saving (rail 2-3 only). Confidence: HIGH — error_class string is deterministic and the STRIKE mechanism already exists; this is a one-class addition.

### F4: Budget cap breaches from estimator headroom not consistently applied
- **Evidence**: 3 of 12 tasks with budget tiers exceeded their caps: homelab#1041 ($0.5245 vs $0.50 sm, 105%), oracle-fleet#355 ($0.7092 vs $0.50 sm, 142%), oracle-fleet#284 ($0.2614 vs $0.25 xs, 105%). All three had calibration_error near or above 1.0 (1.049, 0.355, 1.046). Conversely, 5 tasks were badly under their cap: homelab#1268 ($0.04 of $1.00 md, 4%), sleep-tracking#43 ($0.05 of $1.00 md, 5%), circles#32 ($0.45 of $2.00 lg, 22%). The HEADROOM=2.0 fix from r3 F5 is documented in `estimate_budget.py` but the breaches suggest it's not applied to all code paths or the `pick_tier` function rounds down aggressively.
- **Mechanism**: The `HEADROOM = 2.0` constant is applied to the point estimate, but the tier selection (`pick_tier`) may use a separate code path that doesn't multiply by HEADROOM, or the calibration_error multiplies in after tier selection. A task with calibration_error=1.049 at tier sm ($0.50 cap) would need a raw estimate of $0.25 to fit: estimate × HEADROOM × cal_error = 0.25 × 2.0 × 1.049 = $0.52, which rounds up past sm. If HEADROOM is applied but calibration_error is ignored during tier selection, the breach still occurs.
- **Process change**: In `estimate_budget.py`, add a safety check in `pick_tier`: `assert estimated_cost * HEADROOM * calibration_error < tier_cap` before returning a tier. If the assert would fail, bump to the next tier up. Also add a `--self-test` case that verifies the oracle-fleet#355 scenario (estimated $0.25, HEADROOM 2.0, cal_error 1.05 → must land in md, not sm).
- **Expected saving**: Prevents the 25% breach rate (3/12 tasks). Each breach kills accumulated work — homelab#1041 spent $0.52 across 5 clean rounds before hitting the cap, then needed re-dispatch. The re-dispatch is an operator touch (~€3–8 per in-sitting item on rail 1, plus the wasted $0.52 OpenRouter spend on rail 2). Total: ~€5–10 per breach event. Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH — the breach mechanism is clear from the calibration_error values, but verifying the exact code path requires access to the full `estimate_budget.py` source (only the excerpt was provided).

### F5: repetition-loop not classified as strike-eligible — 5 consecutive rounds = 0 artifact
- **Evidence**: oracle-fleet#304: 5 consecutive harness-death rounds with error_class `repetition-loop`, all on deepseek-v4-flash, all with round=1 (harness retry without advancing round counter). Zero CI runs, zero PR created, issue remains OPEN/blocked. Total active_run_s: 13,165 seconds (3.7h) with no artifact. total_cost_usd: $0.00 (untracked subscription — the real token cost is hidden). No model swap occurred — the coordinator re-dispatched the same model 5 times.
- **Mechanism**: `repetition-loop` is a harness-death exit_status that the coordinator currently treats as retry-eligible (re-dispatch with same model). It is NOT listed in the STRIKE clause. Per the brief: "a second strike with the identical (model, error_class) pair on one issue is not a swap — it routes to the agent/error STRIKE-channel path." But repetition-loop never triggers the strike classification at all, so the "second strike" gate is unreachable. The loop detection exists in the harness (it identifies and kills the session) but the coordinator doesn't recognize it as a model-fitness signal.
- **Process change**: Add `repetition-loop` to the strike error-class list in `README.md` §invariants. On first occurrence: swap model (don't consume a logic round). On second occurrence with same (model, repetition-loop) pair: file AGENT_ERROR and label `agent/error` — don't dispatch a third time. The model-swap from deepseek-v4-flash → haiku is likely to escape the loop since haiku has a 0% repetition-loop rate (0/69 attempts in this ledger).
- **Expected saving**: oracle-fleet#304: collapses 5 rounds to 2 max, saving 3 × 2600s ≈ 2.2h of active compute. Cost saving is on rail 3 (subscription draw, amortized: the token spend is untracked at $0.00 but real; API-equivalent for deepseek-v4-flash: ~$0.10–0.20 per failed round). Main saving is preventing an indefinitely-blocked OPEN issue from occupying a queue slot. Rail 1: €3–8 if the eventual escalation to unblock it is in-sitting. Confidence: HIGH — the error_class string is deterministic.

### F6: Review queue has no timeout — tasks idle for days in agent/review
- **Evidence**: sleep-tracking#123: 302.4h wall time, 299.4h idle (99.7%), only 10,062s active. circles#77: 32.5h wall, 32.1h idle (98.8%). oracle-fleet#279: 84.4h wall, 83.9h idle (99.4%). The idle component dominates: wall_time − active_run_s − queue_wait_s shows that even for agent/done tasks, the majority of wall time is spent waiting for review, not computing. The ledger's wall_time_s is unreliable (not decomposed active/idle), but the PR creation→merge timestamps confirm: circles#77 PR #79 was created Aug 11 and merged Aug 12 — a 32.5h gap mostly from overnight idle.
- **Mechanism**: The `agent/review` state has no TTL. Once a PR is opened and the label flips to `agent/review`, the task sits until the review reflex fires, a human reviews, or the next coordinator tick picks it up. The deterministic scan removes phantom `agent/review` labels (homelab#928), but has no clause for "has been in review > X hours without a verdict."
- **Process change**: Add a review-staleness clause to the deterministic scan (`coordinator-scan.sh`): after `C4C5_PERSIST_S` seconds in `agent/review` without a review verdict (no APPROVED/CHANGES_REQUESTED/COMMENTED on the PR), the scan emits a notification comment pinging the appropriate reviewer channel. If another `C4C5_PERSIST_S` elapses without action, escalate to `agent/blocked` for human attention. This uses existing scan infrastructure — just adds one condition to the C4/C5 predicate.
- **Expected saving**: circles#77's wall drops from 32.5h to ~2h with a 4h ping. Operator time: prevents tasks from being forgotten and re-discovered later — saves ~€20–30 per out-of-sitting summons (rail 1). Rail 2-3: near zero (the compute already happened; the idle costs nothing in billed dollars). Confidence: MEDIUM — the exact idle decomposition is unreliable (ledger blind spot), but the pattern of multi-hour/overnight review gaps is consistent across 10+ tasks.
