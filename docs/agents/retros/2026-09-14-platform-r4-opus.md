# platform loop retro r4 — Claude Opus 5 (1M context)

## Summary (≤5 lines)
The loop's expensive failures are not code failures: 114/156 worker attempts exited clean and 10 of 36 structured tasks never saw a red CI at all, yet still averaged 4.4 rides. What burns money is the **park**: the rounds-exhausted `agent/blocked` park fires on PRs that are converging (homelab#625 parked 16:53:57Z, approved 17:02:42Z, merged 17:10:06Z), and every one of the 8 human un-parks in this window ended in a merge (7/7 issues). The ledger then *re-sells that park as pain* — 8/40 rows are stale snapshots, and 4 of the 8 deep-dive slots (ranks 1, 3, 5, 8) are tasks whose PR merged. Second-order: 44% of strike records carry `error_class=unknown`, so the fleet-fault gate keys on a degenerate value and filed the same issue twice in one day (#1622/#1642). Per the brief I treat `reviewer_rounds`, `wall_time_s`, `retry_storms` and `$0.00` costs as unreliable — stated once here, evidenced from trails throughout.

## Findings (ranked, ≤6)

### F1 — The rounds-exhausted park fires on converging PRs; 7/7 human un-parks ended in a merge (rail 1)
**Evidence.** Across 21 reachable homelab rows: 11 bot parks, **8 human un-parks on 7 issues** (#913, #1041, #625, #791, #876, #1151, #379×2). All 7 subsequently merged — #913→PR915@2026-08-26T16:50:40Z, #1041→PR1058@08-31T05:43:11Z, #625→PR631@08-19T17:10:06Z, #791→PR802@08-23T20:25:24Z, #876→PR896@08-25T17:11:52Z, #1151→PR1208@09-01T21:19:15Z, #379→PR473@08-17T18:55:51Z. Three were **re-parked by the bot after the human un-parked** (#913: park 21:13:16Z → human un-park 06:33:27Z → re-park 07:52:59Z → merged 16:50Z; #625: park 16:38:29Z → un-park 16:42:17Z → re-park 16:53:57Z → **approved 17:02:42Z**; #379 ×2). The park is preceded by scan churn: PR#1058 took **8 `arbitrate` dispatches in 48 minutes** (each with a distinct `state-fp:`), PR#631 5 arbitrate + 1 ci-red in 38 min, PR#915 3 ci-red + 1 arbitrate — against the README's stated "one ride per state change, not one per tick" (homelab#198). PR#915 also spawned 5 reviewer rides that returned no verdict (`STANDING ASIDE` ×4 `pre=checks-pending`, ×1 `pre=own-verdict-at-head`).
**Mechanism (falsifiable).** The rounds-exhausted arbitrate predicate counts rides, not *progress*: ci-red/arbitrate units re-fire while checks are pending, each re-fire advances the ride counter, and the counter crosses the ADR-127 bound while the reviewer's verdict on the current head is still outstanding. Falsify it by finding one park where the head was red **and** the newest reviewer verdict post-dated the last push. I found none in 4 reachable deep-dives. This is already filed live as homelab#1627 (2026-09-13, "the rounds-exhausted arbitrate escalation ignores the PR's Touches footprint and a green+approved …").
**Process change.** README.md §State machine, **`agent/blocked` row**: add a park precondition — *do not park while the PR head's checks are green and the newest reviewer verdict is older than the last push (a verdict is outstanding); re-queue instead.*
**Expected saving.** Rail 1: 8 out-of-sitting un-park summonses over ~5 weeks × E ≈ €20–30 = **€160–240 (~€40/week)**, plus 3 duplicate re-park touches. Rail 3 (amortized, cents): ~14 arbitrate/ci-red coordinator rides and 5 no-verdict reviewer rides on the 4 reachable deep-dives alone.

### F2 — The pain-rank is measuring the emitter, not the loop (rail 1, and it corrupts every future retro)
**Evidence.** Every row the ledger reports as `issue_state=OPEN` is **CLOSED live** — 7/7: #913, #1041, #625, #778, #791, #876, #292. Every `agent/done` row matches live exactly (16/16). Four of the stale rows now carry live `agent/done` (#1041, #791, #876, circles#77) — they are *successes* filed as pain, and they occupy **ranks 3, 9, 10 and 14**. Of the 8 deep-dive slots, ranks 1, 3, 5 and 8 all have merged or operator-closed trails. Repo-wide: **13 of 14 CLOSED homelab issues still carrying `agent/blocked` have ≥1 merged PR referencing them**; two confirmed by PR body — PR#915 (`Implements #913` + `Issue: #913`, base `master`, merged 08-26) and PR#631 (`Fixes #625`, base `master`, merged 08-19), both still `agent/blocked` today, 19 and 26 days on. circles#77 *was* reconciled ("🤖 Stale label reconciled → `agent/done`", homelab#1106) — but on 2026-08-31, **19 days after its 08-12 merge**, and after the ledger snapshot.
**Mechanism.** The C6/`agent/done` reconcile predicate in the state-machine table is gated on `C4C5_PERSIST_S` persistence *and* runs on a belt that homelab's filing volume (132 agent-fix issues in W36) outruns; the ledger emitter reads the label at park time and never re-reads. So a park that a merge later invalidated stays in the ledger forever. Corroborating non-physicality in the same fields: 6 rows have `active_run_s > wall_time_s` (impossible — sleep#71 11733>10055, homelab#778 6974>4772, #270 1377>887, oracle#225 2331>0, sleep#43 1246>0, #148 535>0), and all 12 largest walls have `closed_at` **before** `ts` (homelab#1529: wall 252,264s = 2.9d, closed 263s *before* its own ts; PR1541 opened and merged the same evening).
**Process change.** README.md §State machine, **`agent/done` row**: make the reconcile predicate fire on *closure-by-merge* (issue CLOSED within 60s of a strongly-referencing PR's `merged_at`) **without** waiting out `C4C5_PERSIST_S` — the persist timer exists to avoid racing a live worker, and a merge proves there isn't one.
**Expected saving.** Rail 1: the retro sitting itself — 4 of 8 deep-dive items are false pain (in-sitting, €3–8 each ⇒ **€12–32 per retro**), and the real worst rows are displaced out of the top 8. Also removes the 14-item stale sink that the README says is "a terminal sink invisible to every scan class".

### F3 — 44% of strike records say `error_class=unknown`, and the fleet-fault filing dedups on OPEN issues only (rail 1)
**Evidence.** Of 18 `AGENT_STRIKE:` comments on the 21 reachable homelab rows, **8 say `error_class=unknown`**; the rest mix *exit statuses* into the field (`harness-death` ×4, `budget-403` ×1) alongside real classes (`goose-32602-truncation` ×2, `budget-exhausted` ×2, `budget-exhausted-key` ×1). The ledger's own taxonomy for the same rides is different and richer (`nonzero-exit-1`, `no-output`, `http-401-storm`, `repetition-loop`). Two consequences, both observed:
- **Over-fires by lumping.** homelab#625's `AGENT_ERROR: infra-class strike on 4 issues — error_class=unknown (Go rail dispatched into a HELD capacity latch; strike records name the wrong chain entry)` — the filing itself admits the key is wrong.
- **Under-fires by splitting.** The same budget fault on two distinct issues 106 minutes apart got two different keys: #779 `error_class=budget-403` @2026-08-23T13:58:05Z, #778 `error_class=budget-exhausted` @15:44:21Z. The "≥2 distinct issues inside 24h" predicate could not see them as one fault.
- **Dedup leaks.** homelab#1622 (2026-09-13T15:01:44Z) and #1642 (21:26:08Z) are byte-identical filings — same `error_class: unknown`, same "Affected issues: 1620 1621", both claiming "This is a DEDUPED filing: one per error_class per 24h window". #1622 was CLOSED before 21:26, and the README's dedup clause says "search **open** issues" — so closing a filing re-arms it inside its own window.
**Mechanism.** The STRIKE channel's key is the comment's `error_class=` string, which is emitted by a different code path than the ledger's `error_class` and falls back to `unknown`; the dedup search is scoped to open issues.
**Process change.** README.md §State machine, **`agent/error` row, the "Dedup first, like every filing surface" clause**: change *"search open issues for the same gate/`error_class`"* to *"search open **and closed** issues whose filing timestamp is inside the same 24h window"*, and require the strike emitter to reuse the ledger's `error_class` vocabulary, falling back to `exit_status:<value>` rather than `unknown`.
**Expected saving.** Rail 1: one duplicate out-of-sitting filing on 2026-09-13 alone ⇒ **1 avoidable E (~€20–30)**; and an `unknown`-keyed filing whose whole instruction is "Investigate the platform-level pattern behind `unknown`" costs a full operator sit-down to produce nothing. 44% of strikes currently land in that bucket.

### F4 — The identical-`(model, error_class)` second-strike rule is not enforced: 8 extra rides (rail 3 + wall)
**Evidence.** README invariants: *"a second strike with the identical `(model, error_class)` pair on one issue is not a swap — it routes to the `agent/error` STRIKE-channel path … instead of another ride."* Observed anyway: **oracle-fleet#304 — 5 × (`deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash`, `repetition-loop`), all at round 1**, 13,165s active (3.7h), no PR ever opened, `$0.00` (untracked), terminal `agent/blocked`. Also oracle-fleet#279 3 × `http-401-storm`, #273 2 ×, #397 2 × `goose-32602-truncation`. **8 rides across the ledger that the rule says should not have happened.** Confirmed as a live gap by homelab#1626 (2026-09-13): "router: serving-shaped pair-exclusion (#1234) did not stop a repeat same-pair `goose-32602-truncation`".
**Mechanism.** The pair-exclusion set is held in the dispatching session, and the README's own design rule is "hold no state between actions" — so a re-dispatched round (a different session) re-picks the excluded pair. Falsify by finding a repeat pair *within* one session; all four observed cases span rounds/re-dispatches.
**Process change.** README.md **Invariants paragraph (the MODEL note)**: state that the `(model, error_class)` exclusion set is read from the issue's own `AGENT_STRIKE:` comments at dispatch time, not carried in session — consistent with §Design rule ("state lives in GitHub labels + CRs, never in the coordinator's head").
**Expected saving.** 8 worker rides; on oracle-fleet#304 alone ~10,530s (2.9h) of pod time that produced no artifact. Rail 2: ≈$0 (these rides die before billing); rail 3 amortized: cents. Rail 1: oracle-fleet#304's terminal `agent/blocked` is one avoidable out-of-sitting park (~E) — the STRIKE channel would have filed it instead of summoning.

### F5 — Every over-cap row was estimator-picked; every human-override row landed far under (rail 2)
**Evidence.** 11/40 rows carry a tier at all (**the estimator runs on 28% of dispatches**). Splitting the reachable ones by whether a human `agent-budget/*` label exists:
- **Human override** (homelab#1268 `agent-budget/md`, circles#32 `agent-budget/lg`, circles#19 `agent-budget/lg`): cap utilisation 4%, 22%, 17% — mean 14%, none over cap, best calibration in the ledger (#1268 `calibration_error` 0.006).
- **Estimator-picked** (homelab#1041, #1205, #1151, #1149 — no override label): 105%, 100%, 97%, 72% — mean 93%, **one over cap**. Fleet-wide the other over-cap row is oracle-fleet#355 at **142%** ($0.7092 against a $0.50 `sm` cap).
r3 F5 raised headroom 1.5 → 2.0 to stop exactly this; post-change mean utilisation is **77%** vs 25% before, with 2 over-cap rows where there were none.
**Mechanism.** Headroom is applied to the *estimate*, then `pick_tier` floors into a band — so a headroomed estimate landing just under a band ceiling still yields a cap barely above true spend. Raising the multiplier moves the edge; it doesn't remove it. Falsify by finding an over-cap row whose headroomed estimate sat mid-band; both over-cap rows sit at the `sm` ceiling.
**Process change.** `estimate_budget.py` — **`pick_tier`**: promote to the next tier when the headroomed estimate lands in the top 20% of the chosen tier's cap (a tier-edge guard). Both over-cap rows would have landed in `md` ($1.00). The module's own docstring already licenses this: *"over-sizing slightly beats throttling a legit fix, and an unspent cap costs $0."*
**Expected saving.** Rail 2: prevents the r3 F5 failure mode that is still live — oracle-fleet#1 died at $0.5086 on `403 Key limit exceeded` after 65 min with **zero artifact**. At the observed rate (2 over-cap in 7 estimator-picked rows) this is ~1 lost ride per 4 tiered dispatches; the promotion itself costs $0 when unspent. Rail 1: each mid-flight 403 ends in a park (~E).

### F6 — `deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash` as the round-1 default is a coin flip (rail 3 + wall)
**Evidence.** As the **first** model of a task it infra-strikes **13/21 = 62%**; haiku **1/7 = 14%**. Attempt-level across all 156 rides: deepseek 27/67 bad (40%) — `harness-death` 11, `auth-storm` 6, `failed` 5, `budget-403` 3 — vs haiku 9/79 (11%), and haiku's 9 are 6 `ci-failed` (a *logic* outcome) plus 2 `failed` and 1 deliberate stop. **9 tasks show the exact strike→swap→clean shape**: homelab#625, #1205, #1151, #892, #779, #866, #617, oracle-fleet#272, #273. Overall **33/156 rides (21%) are infra strikes**, each paying a pod spawn plus the round's queue wait (median 406s, mean 529s).
**Mechanism.** The failure modes are rail-shaped, not capability-shaped: all 9 `goose-32602-truncation`, all 6 `http-401-storm` and all 4 `budget-403` land on OpenRouter-routed deepseek variants, none on haiku. The model is fine; the route under it isn't. Falsify by finding a `goose-32602-truncation` or `http-401-storm` on a non-OpenRouter-routed rail — none in 156 rides.
**Process change.** `estimate_budget.py` §Usage — the dispatch pipeline's hard-coded `--model openrouter/deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash`: on any round after a strike on this issue, pass the model that produced the last **clean** ride on this repo instead of the fixed default.
**Expected saving.** ~13 of 21 first-attempt strikes avoided ⇒ 13 pod spawns and ~13 × 529s ≈ **1.9h of wall** per 21 tasks, and it removes the round-1 strike that precedes 3 of the F1 parks. **Honest accounting:** this is *not* a cash saving — it moves spend from rail 2 (billed OpenRouter dollars, currently $7.21 tracked across 40 rows) to rail 3 (subscription draw: cash-amortized ≈ cents under headroom, API-equivalent higher). The win is wall-time and avoided parks, not dollars.
