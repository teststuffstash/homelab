# 2026-11-20 — nx-02's boot SSD dropped off the bus; the dead backup target filled Postgres with WAL

**Status: DRAFT.** nx-02 rebooted 2026-11-21 08:49Z (§Recovery); the router pair is parked with
pve as MASTER after a WAN outage on take-back. The lessons below are open questions for that session, not rulings.
**Tracked by:** FU-299 (backups) until the morning session routes the lessons.

**Impact.**
- Grafana was down from about 16:33Z on 11-20 until 00:25Z on 11-21, because `grafana-pg`'s
  primary filled its 2 GiB volume with unarchived WAL.
- `forgejo-pg` and `infisical-pg` were about 9 h from the same end. Infisical full would mean
  secrets and ESO stop working.
- `oracle-pg` (oracle-fleet) is not archiving either, and its volume is 2 GiB. Its write rate is
  low: WAL grew about 80 MB in the 25 h to 11-21 07:50Z, with 1.3 GB free, so it has about two
  weeks. Last night's volume grows missed it.
- No Longhorn backups and no Postgres WAL or base backups have been written since about 06:50Z
  on 11-20. The router pair, cp-02, wk-04, ci-runner-02 and opnsense-test kept running the whole
  time, because their disks are on nx-02's NVMe, not the SA400.

**Root cause.** A single consumer SATA SSD is nx-02's only boot disk and also holds the
off-cluster backup target. It detached from the bus, and reseating it brought it back, so the
contact, cable or bay was suspect. The 11-21 diagnosis below leans toward the drive's controller.
Postgres then turned "backup target down" into "production database down",
because CNPG keeps every unarchived WAL segment and the volumes were sized with no headroom for
that.

**Disk or bus? (diagnosed 2026-11-21 ~07:30Z, before the reboot.)** The most likely cause is a
controller hang in the drive: it stopped answering commands, and pulling it out and back in
(which also power-cycles it) cleared the hang. The flash and the link both read clean. This is
not proven. The kernel ring buffer had already wrapped (journald's read-only spam fills it in
about 11 min), so there are no kernel messages from the event. The evidence:
- **The stall (Prometheus `pve-node`, `sda`).** Between the 06:47:19 and 06:48:19 scrapes, busy
  time grew 32 s (65 s over the 2-min window) and write wait grew 589 s, while only about 280 I/Os
  completed. That is one stall the length of the 30 s SCSI command timeout. The device was gone
  by 06:49:19.
- **No warning beforehand.** There is no earlier stall in the 16.5 days Prometheus holds. Busy
  time above 8 s per 2 min only appears at the nightly 02:02 load, and those windows completed
  25k–115k writes.
- **The signal path** (hardware repo `docs/nx-6035-g5.md`; the 6-bays-per-node split comes from
  the market read and the bay-to-PHY map is not recorded):
  - The drive and its caddy plug into a bay connector on the chassis backplane. The bays take
    their power from the backplane, which the shared PSUs feed.
  - Signals cross the backplane to nx-02's tray connector (each node tray slots into the
    backplane from the rear), then go over the board to the onboard SAS3008's PHY 0.
  - The operator reseated only the drive. That power-cycled it and re-mated the drive-to-bay
    contact. It did not touch the tray connector, the backplane or the HBA.
- **nx-01 was quiet.** nx-01 (same chassis) shows no I/O stall in that minute. That rules out the
  shared PSUs and the backplane as a whole. It says nothing about nx-02's own tray connector.
- **nx-02's tray connector is the least likely link.**
  - A fault on PHY 0's lane there would not be cured by reseating the drive, and PHY 0 has been
    clean since.
  - The spare, in another bay (PHY 5), trained at 6 Gb/s through the same tray connector while
    the original was gone and ran clean. That cannot rule out a single-lane fault.
  - The earlier "bay disks missing after seating a blade" episodes were nx-01 (09-15, 09-24). They
    were explained by a dead CMOS cell resetting `LSI HBA OPROM` to Disabled. That is a BIOS
    boot-visibility effect, not a lost link. nx-02's VBAT reads 3.11 V.
  - Caveat: since the reseat the drive has carried no load (nothing is mounted).
- **Flash and controller are clean** (`smartctl -x`, static binary run from `/dev/shm`).
  0 uncorrectables, 0 program/erase failures, an empty error log, 99 % life left, and the
  extended self-test passed at 3251 h.
- **The link is clean on the drive side.** Interface CRC errors (Device Statistics and attribute
  218) read 1 for the drive's whole life, unchanged across the 17-day node-exporter history and
  across the event. Attribute 199 is not a counter: its raw value flips between multiples of
  65536 every 15 min.
- **The only evidence for the bus is on the HBA side.** The LSI3008 (FW 16.00.10.00) PHY 0,
  where `sdc` sits, shows 29 invalid dwords, 24 disparity errors and 1 loss of dword sync. PHY 5
  (the spare, hot-inserted) shows 0. The counters were unchanged over about 30 min of reads and
  through the self-test. The drive's PHY event log since the reseat shows 1 PhyRdy→PhyNRdy and
  2 COMRESETs (the spare: 0 and 1). The operator pulling a drive with a live link can produce
  these numbers. Ongoing signal-integrity trouble would also show up as drive-side CRC errors,
  and there are none.
- **What would settle it.**
  - After the reboot, read `journalctl -k -b -1 --since "2026-11-20 06:45" --until "06:50"`
    (times in UTC). The stall recovered at about 06:48 while root was still writable, so
    mpt3sas's timeout and reset lines may have reached disk. Their `log_info` codes tell a
    device timeout from a PHY or link loss.
  - If it happens again, swap bays: the original drive into another bay, a different drive into
    its old bay. A fault that follows the bay points at the bay or the lane. One that follows the
    drive points at the drive. The 09-24 nx-01 diagnosis was settled the same way. The 11-21
    reseat went back into the same bay (operator), so that swap has not been done yet.
  - Re-read PHY 0's counters at the next maintenance (`/sys/class/sas_phy/phy-0:0/*_count`). If
    they grow while the drive runs, it is the link.

**Lessons (for the morning, not yet routed).**
1. **A missing backup must not take down production.** Unarchived WAL grows without bound.
   Options to weigh: a cap on WAL kept for archiving (let the archive fall behind and alert,
   rather than fill the disk), volume headroom sized to N days of WAL at the measured rate, or
   both. That is a decision about which we lose first: backups or service.
2. **Single boot disk on a hypervisor.** nx-02 (and pve?) boot from one consumer SSD. A mirror
   (ZFS/mdadm) or a SATADOM pair, plus UEFI boot (the spike already calls that the structural
   fix), would have kept this a degraded-array alert.
3. **The backup target shares a fate with a hypervisor's boot disk.** LXC 220 sits on the same
   SA400 as nx-02's root. Weigh a separate disk, or a second target.
4. **Detection did not reach a human in time.** The causal alerts fired at about 07:00 as
   `warning` and sat for about 15 h among about 50 standing alerts. Ask which of
   `LonghornBackupTargetDown`, CNPG archiving-failed and WAL-growth should page, and why there
   is no alert on `ContinuousArchiving=False` itself, if that is the case (check the
   pg-backup rules).
5. **Fill-rate alerting.** `KubePersistentVolumeFillingUp` went critical only 20 min before the
   outage. A WAL-growth or time-to-full alert sized on the archive-failure case would have given
   hours.

**Recovery (2026-11-21, attended, window `seat-1795249029-524`).**
- The operator pulled the spare. `host-maint down nx-02 --reboot` failed at its first `qm shutdown`:
  Proxmox writes a task log under `/var/log/pve/tasks` first, and root was read-only. So the seat
  did the rest by hand:
  - QMP `system_powerdown` for ci-runner-02 and opnsense-test.
  - `node-maintenance down` for wk-04, then for cp-02 once Longhorn was healthy again (~16 min).
  - LXC 220 left running; its disk was gone.
  - `carp-maintenance nx02 enter`: pve took `.1` at 08:49Z with a ~5 s WAN gap.
  - QMP powerdown of the nx-02 router, then `echo b > /proc/sysrq-trigger` at 08:49:50Z.
- nx-02 was back on ssh after 137 s, root read-write on the SA400 (now `sda`). `host-maint up`:
  cp-02 and wk-04 Ready, etcd 3/3, cilium 13/13, ci-runner-02 undrained, LXC 220 running,
  `/health` 200, Longhorn backup target available. All four CNPG clusters show
  `ContinuousArchiving=True`.
- The previous boot's journal ends at 06:47:12Z, the start of the stall, so no kernel line from
  the event reached disk. §Disk or bus stays unsettled; the bay swap is the remaining test.
- **WAN outage 08:54:10–09:02:33Z (~8 min).** `host-maint up` ran `carp-maintenance leave`.
  nx-02 preempted back to MASTER, its WAN gate raised the link, and dhclient got the lease

  `carp-maintenance nx02 enter` again: pve MASTER, internet back at 09:02:33Z. The dead-man was
  then disarmed, so nx-02 stays parked. Cause not found. The one recorded nx-02 take-back
