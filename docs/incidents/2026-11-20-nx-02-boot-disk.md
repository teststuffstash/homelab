# 2026-11-20 — nx-02's boot SSD dropped off the bus; the dead backup target filled Postgres with WAL

**Status: DRAFT (2026-11-21 ~00:45Z).** nx-02 is not rebooted yet; that happens in the morning,
attended. The lessons below are open questions for that session, not rulings.
**Tracked by:** FU-299 (backups) until the morning session routes the lessons.

**Impact.**
- Grafana was down from about 16:33Z on 11-20 until 00:25Z on 11-21, because `grafana-pg`'s
  primary filled its 2 GiB volume with unarchived WAL.
- `forgejo-pg` and `infisical-pg` were about 9 h from the same end. Infisical full would mean
  secrets and ESO stop working.
- No Longhorn backups and no Postgres WAL or base backups have been written since about 06:50Z
  on 11-20. The router pair, cp-02, wk-04, ci-runner-02 and opnsense-test kept running the whole
  time, because their disks are on nx-02's NVMe, not the SA400.

**Timeline (UTC).**

- 11-20 ~06:50 nx-02's Kingston SA400 (the host's only boot disk: Proxmox root, swap, and the
  700 G `local-lvm` that holds backup Garage LXC 220) disappears from the bus.
  - `/sys/block` no longer lists it.
  - `pve-root` goes to `emergency_ro`, and any binary not already in page cache fails with
    `Input/output error`.
  - The CNPG `ContinuousArchiving` condition goes False on forgejo-pg, infisical-pg and
    grafana-pg at 06:50:25–31 (barman-cloud-wal-archive, exit 4).
  - The node exporter's textfile metrics stop refreshing at the same time.
- 07:02 `PveMetricsStale` (nx-02) fires, then `RouterWanGateSilent` and `EndpointProbeFailing`
  on `192.168.2.73:3903`, and at 07:18 `LonghornBackupTargetDown`. All are severity
  `warning`. Nobody acted on them for about 15 h.
- 09:39 `MgmtBeltCheckFailing` `tofu:provisioning` fires. Likely the same cause: the
  provisioning root owns LXC 220. Not verified.
- From about 07:00 WAL accumulates at about 200 MB/h per cluster (measured with `pg_ls_waldir()`
  at 23:50Z: 1168 MB in the last 6 h, 208 MB in the last 1 h).
- 16:13 `KubePersistentVolumeFillingUp` (critical, m70s). At 16:33 `CNPGNoWritablePrimary`
  fires: grafana-pg-3 hits low disk, releases the primary lease and crash-loops. Grafana itself
  crash-loops after it, and the `grafana.teststuff.net` probe fails.
- 22:3x The operator asks the seat to look at the alerts. Reading the alert list leads to
  grafana-pg ("Not enough disk space"), then to the archiving failure, then to nx-02 over ssh,
  where `df`/`dmesg` return I/O errors, `lsblk` shows only the NVMe and `local-lvm` is inactive.
- 23:5x The seat grows the volumes: forgejo-pg and infisical-pg 5→15 GiB, grafana-pg 2→6 GiB
  (`85b7c85c`). forgejo-pg was also patched live, because tofu owns it. The grafana PVCs needed
  a direct patch: CNPG did not resize them while the cluster was unhealthy.
- 00:0x The operator hot-plugs a spare SA400 (480 GB, previously used by Talos) into a free
  nx-02 bay. It enumerates as `sdb`. The seat wipes its first and last 2 MiB with perl (the only
  writer that still ran from page cache; wipefs, dd and sgdisk all failed with I/O errors) and
  verified the zeros by reading them back, so it cannot boot nx-02 into a stale Talos.
- 00:25 grafana-pg is back with grafana-pg-4 promoted; Grafana `/api/health` returns 200.
- 00:3x The operator reseats the original SA400. It re-enumerates as `sdc` (894 GiB) with its
  partitions intact (BIOS-boot, ESP, LVM). The running kernel cannot rebind root to it, so a
  reboot is required. **Deferred to the morning, attended.**

**Root cause.** A single consumer SATA SSD is nx-02's only boot disk and also holds the
off-cluster backup target. It detached from the bus, and reseating it brought it back, so the
contact, cable or bay is suspect. Whether the disk itself is failing is unknown; read SMART
after the reboot. Postgres then turned "backup target down" into "production database down",
because CNPG keeps every unarchived WAL segment and the volumes were sized with no headroom for
that.

**Lessons (for the morning, not yet routed).**
1. **A missing backup must not take down production.** Unarchived WAL grows without bound.
   Options to weigh: a cap on WAL kept for archiving (let the archive fall behind and alert,
   rather than fill the disk), volume headroom sized to N days of WAL at the measured rate, or
   both. That is a decision about which we lose first: backups or service.
2. **Single boot disk on a hypervisor.** nx-02 (and pve?) boot from one consumer SSD. A mirror
   (ZFS/mdadm) or a SATADOM pair, plus UEFI boot (the spike already calls that the structural
   fix), would have kept this a degraded-array alert.
3. **The backup target shares a fate with a hypervisor's boot disk.** LXC 220 sits on the same
   SA400 as nx-02's root. Weigh a separate disk, or a second target.
4. **Detection did not reach a human in time.** The causal alerts fired at about 07:00 as
   `warning` and sat for about 15 h among about 50 standing alerts. Ask which of
   `LonghornBackupTargetDown`, CNPG archiving-failed and WAL-growth should page, and why there
   is no alert on `ContinuousArchiving=False` itself, if that is the case (check the
   pg-backup rules).
5. **Fill-rate alerting.** `KubePersistentVolumeFillingUp` went critical only 20 min before the
   outage. A WAL-growth or time-to-full alert sized on the archive-failure case would have given
   hours.

**Recovery still owed.**
- Pull the spare disk.
- Shut the nx-02 guests down cleanly and reboot.
- Verify the router pair (CARP), cp-02 and wk-04 Ready, LXC 220 serving, `ContinuousArchiving`
  True and the WAL draining, and Longhorn backups resuming.
- Read SMART on the SA400.
- Decide whether the volume grows stay or revert. They cannot shrink, so they stay unless the
  clusters are rebuilt.
